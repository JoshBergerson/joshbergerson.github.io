// UCM Motorsport / 2026 / In Progress
600V Pouch Cell
Accumulator

As Electrical Lead for UC Motorsport, I am designing and building the tractive battery pack for the team's fully electric Formula Student race car. After a decade of cylindrical cell designs, I am pioneering UCM's first accumulator built around pouch cell technology. The shift offers significant performance gains but demands equally substantial engineering rigour, with every aspect designed from the ground up and no internal precedent to draw from.

Key Engineering Decisions

Cell Tab Connections, Rather than welding, I designed a clamped tab connection system that creates a low-resistance electrical joint while keeping the pack fully serviceable in a racing environment.

Module Housing, UL94-V0 rated insulating foam and 25% glass-filled nylon housing, with cells compressed to 20 psi for consistent electrochemical performance.

Control Architecture, Relay and tractive fuse selection, precharge circuitry, voltage indication, and BMS integration, all governed by Formula Student safety regulations.

I also authored the Pouch Cell Handling Handbook for the University of Canterbury, establishing safe handling protocols that will remain with the team beyond this season.

// University of Canterbury / Tonga / Nov 2025 – Feb 2026
Biomedical Engineering
Solutions in Tonga

Selected as part of an eight-strong UC engineering team deployed to Tonga for one month with a single goal: tangibly improve the healthcare system. My focus was sterilisation capability, working with one teammate to restore broken autoclaves and train local staff to use donated ones sitting unused in storage.

Resourceful Repair

One early find was a TOMY SX-500 laboratory autoclave sitting unused in storage. With no fault history and no documentation, we diagnosed a seized door interlock actuator, freed it, and 3D printed a replacement component using the printer we brought. It was returned to service the same day.

Nurse Training

Several donated autoclaves sat unused simply because no one had been trained to use them. We developed a structured training programme and delivered it to approximately 30 nurses across multiple facilities, designing it to be passed on after we left.

Beyond the Brief

The team also completed a significant cleanup of the Vei-Tongo storage facility, which had accumulated a backlog of medical waste. Real impact required working beyond the traditional engineering brief.

// University of Canterbury / RoboCup 2025 / 1st Place
RoboCup
Winning Robot

A full-year project competed by all third-year Mechatronics students. Forty teams of three design and build a fully autonomous robot for a round-robin competition. My robot placed first in 2025, built around a crop harvester-inspired sorting mechanism and a software architecture designed for reliability over complexity.

Unified Control Architecture

Most teams wrote hard-coded logic for each scenario, an approach that breaks at edge cases. I designed a single state-machine controller fed with different error signals depending on the state: wall proximity in AvoidWall, distance-to-weight in Pickup, and IMU heading in ReturnHome. The same control logic handled every situation. The robot never got stuck and returned home reliably, sometimes multiple times per round.

The Sorting Mechanism

A custom kicker geometry, iterated specifically to redirect weights reliably regardless of approach angle, also proved capable of catching the Snitch, a small rolling robot notoriously difficult to intercept. The robot captured it 3 out of 7 rounds, three times more than any other team.

// University of Canterbury / Solar Car Challenge / 1st Place 2025
Solar Car
Race Winner

A half-year project for Electrical and Mechatronics students at UC. Teams design and build a buck converter to drive a solar-powered race car, with efficiency as the primary metric. Working in a team of three, we placed first in 2025.

The Key Decision: MOSFET Gate Drive

Every competing team used a BJT-based switching design, straightforward to implement but carrying higher switching losses at the required frequencies. We chose a MOSFET gate drive configuration, which offered significantly lower on-resistance and faster switching characteristics. This single decision was the primary differentiator between our design and the field, and the efficiency gains it delivered were reflected in the final result.

Skills

Designed and iterated the buck converter from schematic through to physical PCB in KiCad, developing practical skills in component selection, trace routing, and board-level optimisation for a switching power application.
