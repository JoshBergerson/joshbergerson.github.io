// UCM — 2026 — In Progress
600V Pouch Cell Accumulator

As Electrical Team Lead for UC Motorsport, I am pioneering the first pouch cell accumulator ever used in a UCM electric race car. This is a significant departure from the cylindrical cell format used in previous years and represents a step-change in energy density and packaging flexibility.

The Challenge

Pouch cells offer superior energy density but require more careful mechanical design — they're more susceptible to swelling and need robust clamping systems. Designing a pack that is structurally sound, thermally managed, and competition-legal at 600V with no precedent at UCM made this a first-principles engineering challenge.

My Role

I am responsible for cell selection, module layout, BMS integration, electrical isolation, and the overall pack architecture. The vehicle will compete internationally in China and Australia in 2026.

// Formula Student Australasia — 1st Place 2025
Formula Student
Australasia 2025

In 2025, UC Motorsport took 1st Place at the Formula Student Australasia Competition — the result of a full year of design, build, late nights, and an entire team refusing to accept anything less than competition-ready.

Scrutineering

Scrutineering is where Formula Student is won or lost before a wheel turns. Every electrical system — high-voltage interlocks, insulation resistance, shutdown circuits, harness routing — must pass rigorous technical inspection by competition officials. Any fault means the car can't race.

In the lead-up to and during competition, I led the electrical team through long nights in the workshop diagnosing issues, chasing faults, and getting every system to standard. The pressure is real — there's no hiding a half-finished car at scrutineering. We passed. The car ran. We won.

My Electrical Scope

Full electrical architecture: high-voltage tractive system design, harness layout and build, interlock circuits, BMS integration, and on-site fault diagnosis during competition. Race day demands you solve problems in minutes that would normally take days.

The Result

1st place overall against university teams from across Australia and New Zealand — and the foundation for the 2026 electric programme now underway.

// UC Solar Car Challenge — 1st Place 2025
Solar Car
Buck Converter PCB

Designed a custom PCB for the power management system of UC's solar car entry in the 2025 Solar Car Challenge. Our team's key innovation was using MOSFET switching to improve efficiency in the buck converter — a design choice that outperformed other teams' approaches and contributed directly to the 1st place result.

PCB Design

The design involved selecting appropriate MOSFETs for the switching topology, laying out the PCB to minimise parasitic inductance in the switching loop, and validating the design through simulation and physical testing. The efficiency gain over a diode-based approach was measurable and meaningful at competition scale.

Key Skills

PCB layout and design, power electronics, MOSFET selection and gate drive, schematic capture, and physical prototyping and testing.

// Vaiola Hospital, Tonga — Nov 2025 – Feb 2026
Biomedical Engineering
Tonga

Deployed as part of an 8-person UC biomedical engineering team to strengthen healthcare infrastructure in Tonga, working directly with staff at Vaiola Hospital and local clinics over a month-long placement.

The Problem

Vaiola Hospital had only one functioning autoclave on the main island — the sterilisation equipment critical for surgical safety. Multiple units had failed over time with no spare parts available and limited technical support in-country.

The Solution

Through systematic fault diagnosis, creative improvisation, and close collaboration with Tongan biomedical technicians, we increased the number of operational autoclaves from one to six. Every repair had to be sustainable — designed to be maintained by local staff long after we left.

What It Taught Me

Engineering in a zero-resource environment strips away every comfort. You can't order parts, you can't Google the fix, and the stakes are real. It taught me more about problem-solving and resourcefulness than any lab ever could.
