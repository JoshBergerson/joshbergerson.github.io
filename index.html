// UCM — 2026 — In Progress
600V Pouch Cell
Accumulator

As Electrical Team Lead for UC Motorsport, I am pioneering the first pouch cell accumulator ever used in a UCM electric race car — a significant departure from the cylindrical cell format used in previous years, representing a step-change in energy density and packaging flexibility.

The Challenge

Pouch cells offer superior energy density but require more careful mechanical design — susceptible to swelling and needing robust clamping. Designing a pack that is structurally sound, thermally managed, and competition-legal at 600V with no internal precedent made this a first-principles engineering challenge.

My Role

Cell selection, module layout, BMS integration, electrical isolation, and overall pack architecture. The vehicle will compete internationally in China and Australia in 2026.

// Vaiola Hospital, Tonga — Nov 2025 – Feb 2026
Biomedical Engineering
Solutions in Tonga

Deployed as part of an 8-person UC biomedical engineering team to strengthen healthcare infrastructure in Tonga, working directly with staff at Vaiola Hospital and local clinics over a month-long placement.

The Problem

Vaiola Hospital had only one functioning autoclave on the main island — sterilisation equipment critical for surgical safety. Multiple units had failed over time with no spare parts available and limited technical support in-country.

The Solution

Through systematic fault diagnosis, creative improvisation, and close collaboration with Tongan biomedical technicians, we increased the number of operational autoclaves from one to six. Every repair had to be sustainable — designed to be maintained by local staff long after we left.

What It Taught Me

Engineering in a zero-resource environment strips away every comfort. You can't order parts, you can't Google the fix, and the stakes are real.

Vaiola Hospital, Tonga

// UC Solar Car Challenge — 1st Place 2025
Solar Car
Race Winner

Designed a custom PCB for the power management system of UC's solar car entry. Our key innovation was using MOSFET switching to improve efficiency in the buck converter — outperforming other teams' approaches and contributing directly to the 1st place result.

PCB Design

MOSFET selection for the switching topology, PCB layout to minimise parasitic inductance, and validation through simulation and physical testing. The efficiency gain over a diode-based approach was measurable at competition scale.
